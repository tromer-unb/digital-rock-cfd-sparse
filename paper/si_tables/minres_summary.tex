The criterion holds for 14 of the 15 runs, and the largest difference is $1.2\times10^{-3}$. MINRES needed between 2,936 and 6,057 iterations, a count that grows by a factor of 1.3 to 1.5 for each configuration while the number of unknowns grows 7- to 8-fold, and every run stopped through the backward-error test of equation~\eqref{eq:test1}. The true relative residual of the accepted solutions lies between $2.1\times10^{-5}$ and $1.2\times10^{-3}$, two to four orders of magnitude above the requested tolerance, as equation~\eqref{eq:trueres} anticipates. MINRES took 5 to 26 times longer than sparse LU on the same matrix, and on the two finest levels it needed 2.4 to 3.1 times less memory. The algebraic error of MINRES follows its true residual, and the one run that missed the criterion, R3-V at level 5, also has the largest true residual. The direct solver is therefore the faster and more accurate choice whenever its memory fits, and MINRES the choice that bounds memory, provided that its true residual is tested.
